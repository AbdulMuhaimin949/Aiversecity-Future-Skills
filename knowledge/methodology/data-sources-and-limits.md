# Aiversecity data sources and known limitations

## Sources

- Malaysia Standard Classification of Occupations (MASCO) 2020, Ministry of Human Resources (KESUMA): occupation codes and titles. The eMASCO job directory portal is also referenced.
- Malaysia Critical Occupations List (MyCOL) 2022/2023 and MyMahir MyCOL 2024/2025, TalentCorp and ILMIA: which occupations are in critical shortage.
- ILMIA and DOSM My Job Profile, and PERKESO MYFutureJobs Labour Market Exchange: labour-market context.
- ESCO v1.2.1, European Commission: the skills taxonomy that the role skill lists are aligned with.
- HRD Corp e-LATiH: free online courses for Malaysians, used for reskilling pathways.
- United Nations Sustainable Development Goals: each role and skill is linked to SDG goals and targets.
- ILO Working Paper 96 (2023), Generative AI and Jobs: used only for the relative ordering of AI exposure across sectors.
- Aiversecity synthetic data generator (seed 42): synthetic CVs, test profiles and simulated demand trends.

## Limitations

- Demand trend data is simulated and anchored to MyCOL; it is labelled as an estimate.
- The learning catalogue has only 14 courses, mostly for technology and core skills, so many skill gaps, including all gaps specific to non-technology sectors, are shown as uncovered.
- Role descriptions and proficiency targets are generated by the Aiversecity pipeline. A source-attributed record cites a public source but not every field was checked against it.
- The CV scanner reads text-based PDFs only; scanned or photographed CVs cannot be read.
- Aiversecity is a decision-support tool. It does not make hiring decisions, and its scores are estimates, not guarantees.
